\chapter{G-formula (Sequential Backdoor
Adjustment Formula)}
\label{ch-g-formula}

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=5in]
{g-formula/piscina-mirabilis.jpeg}
\caption{Piscina Mirabilis,
ancient Roman cistern in Naples}
\label{fig-piscina}
\end{figure}


This chapter is based
on Ref.\cite{pearl-robins-95}
by Pearl and Robins.


The goal of this
chapter is to
generalize
the backdoor adjustment
formula (see Chapter \ref{ch-bdoor})
from a single do-query $P(y|\cald\rvx=x)$
to multiple do-queries
$P(y|\cald\rvx^n=x^n)$.
The resulting generalized adjustment
formula is called  the {\bf sequential backdoor (SBD)
adjustment formula},
or simply the {\bf
g-formula}, where the \qt{g} stands for generalized.

The {\bf g-formula}
defines recursively the full
probability distribution of a bnet.
In other words, it's a recursive
definition of a Dynamical Bayesian Network.\footnote{Dynamical
Bayesian Networks
are discussed in Chapter \ref{ch-dyn-bnets}.}


Suppose

\begin{align}
P(y,  x^{K+1}, c^{K+1}) &=
P(x_0)P(c_1)
P(y|c_{K+1}, x_K)
\prod_{k=1}^K
P(x_k | c_k, x_{k-1})
P(c_{k+1}|c_k, x_k)
\\
&=
P(x_0)P(c_1)
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_{K+1}\ar[d]
\\
y&x_K\ar[l]
}
\ecen
\prod_{k=1}^K
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_{k+1}
&c_k\ar@[red][d]
\ar[l]
\\
&x_k\ar[lu] &x_{k-1}\ar@[red][l]
}\ecen
\end{align}

For $K=1$, this gives
\beq
P(y, x^2, c^2)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_{2}\ar[d]
&\PP c_1\ar@[red][d]
\ar[l]
\\
y&x_1\ar[lu]\ar[l] &\PP x_{0}\ar@[red][l]
}\ecen
\eeq

For $K=2$, this gives
\beq
P(y, x^3, c^3)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_3\ar[d]
&
c_{2}\ar[l]\ar@[red][d]
&\PP c_1\ar@[red][d]
\ar[l]
\\
y
&x_2\ar[ul]\ar[l]
&x_1\ar[lu]\ar@[red][l] &\PP x_{0}\ar@[red][l]
}\ecen
\eeq


For $K=3$, this gives
\beq
P(y, x^4, c^4)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_4\ar[d]
&c_3\ar@[red][d]\ar[l]
&
c_{2}\ar[l]\ar@[red][d]
&\PP c_1\ar@[red][d]
\ar[l]
\\
y
&x_3\ar[lu]\ar[l]
&x_2\ar[ul]\ar@[red][l]
&x_1\ar[lu]\ar@[red][l] &\PP x_{0}\ar@[red][l]
}\ecen
\label{eq-adj-mediator}
\eeq

From here on, we will speak about the $K=3$ case only.
One can easily generalize what we shall 
say to other values of $K$.

There are no hidden variables in
this scenario, so to get do's on $\rvx_1, \rvx_2$ and $\rvx_3$,
we simply amputate the 
arrows entering those nodes (which
we have highlighted in red)
\beq
P(y|\cald \rvx_1=x_1,
\cald \rvx_2 = x_2,
\cald \rvx_3 = x_3)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
\sum c_4\ar[d]
&\sum c_3\ar[l]
&\sum c_{2}\ar[l]
&\EE c_1
\ar[l]
\\
y
&x_3\ar[lu]\ar[l]
&x_2\ar[ul]\
&x_1\ar[lu]
}\ecen
\eeq

Note that 
if we reverse the arrows 
connecting nodes $\rvc_{k+1}$ and $\rvx_k$,
the $\rvc_k$ become confounders
instead of mediators.
In that scenario, we get

\beq
P(y, x^4, c^4)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
c_4\ar[d]\ar@[red][dr]
&c_3\ar@[red][d]\ar[l]\ar@[red][dr]
&
c_{2}\ar[l]\ar@[red][d]\ar@[red][dr]
&\PP c_1\ar@[red][d]
\ar[l]
\\
y
&x_3\ar[l]
&x_2\ar@[red][l]
&x_1\ar@[red][l] &\PP x_{0}\ar@[red][l]
}\ecen
\label{eq-adj-confounder}
\eeq
and
\beq
P(y|\cald \rvx_1=x_1,
\cald \rvx_2 = x_2,
\cald \rvx_3 = x_3)=
\bcen
\xymatrix{
\sum c_4\ar[d]
&\sum c_3\ar[l]
&\sum c_{2}\ar[l]
&\EE c_1
\ar[l]
\\
y
&x_3\ar[l]
&
&
}\ecen
\eeq

It's possible to add more arrows to
the bnets Eq.(\ref{eq-adj-mediator})
and Eq.(\ref{eq-adj-confounder}).
For example, one can add arrows
\begin{itemize}
\item from all the $\rvc_k$ 
and all the $\rvx_k$ to $\rvy$.
\item
from  $\rvc_{k-1}, \rvc_{k-2}, \ldots, \rvc_1$ to $\rvc_k$ for all $k$
\item
from  $\rvx_{k-1}, \rvx_{k-2}, \ldots, \rvx_0$ to $\rvx_k$ for all $k$
\end{itemize}
Since there are no hidden variables,
an obvious adjustment formula always exists.
