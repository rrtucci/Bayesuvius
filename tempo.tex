polar coordinates parametrization of $B_2(R)$
\beqa
x&=& r\cos\theta\\
y&=& r\sin\theta
\eeqa

\beq
x_1^2 + x_2^2 = r^2(\cos^2\theta
+
\sin^2\theta)= r^2
\eeq

\beqa
dx&=& dr\cos\theta -r \sin\theta \;d\theta
\\
y&=& dr\sin\theta + \cos\theta \;d\theta
\eeqa

\beq
(ds)^2 = (dx_1)^2 + (dx_2)^2 =
(dr)^2 + (rd\theta)^2
\eeq

Spherical (all angles) parametrization of $\partial B_2(R)= S_1(R)$. Set $r=R$ 
in $B_2(R)$ parametrization to
get $S_1(R)$ parametrization 
\beqa
x&=&R \cos \theta
\\
y&=&R \sin\theta
\eeqa
with $r\in (0, R]$ and $\theta\in [0, 2\pi]$.
Set $dr=0$ and $r=R$ in $B_2(R)$
metric to get $S_1(R)$ metric.

\beq
(ds)^2 = R^2 (d\theta)^2
\eeq

Parametrization of $B_4(R)$

Abbreviations
\beq
C_i = \cos\theta_i,
\quad
S_i = \sin\theta_i
\eeq
\begin{subequations}
\beqa
x_1 &=& rC_1
\\
x_2 &=& r S_1 C_2
\\
x_3 &=& rS_1 S_2 C_3
\\
x_4 &=& r S_1 S_2 S_3
\eeqa
\label{eq-param-normal}
\end{subequations}
where $r\in (0, R]$, $\theta_1, \theta_2\in[0, \pi]$ and $\theta_3\in [0, 2\pi]$.

It's easy  to see that for this  parametrization 
\beq
(x_1)^2 + (x_2)^2 + (x_3)^2 + (x_4)
^2 = r^2
\eeq

After calculating the differentials of the $dx_i$ and adding their 
squares, cross-terms cancel and we arrive at

\beq
(ds)^2 =\sum_{i=1}^4 (dx_i)^2=
(dr)^2+(r d\theta_1)^2+(r S_1d\theta_2)^2+(r S_1 S_2d\theta_3)^2
\eeq

Coordinates are said to be orthogonal
if their metric $(ds)^2$ is diagonal (has no cross terms).
If the metric is
diagonal, and all the coefficients 
are 1 as in the rectangular metric $(ds)^2=\sum_i(dx_i))^2$,
then the coordinates are said to be orthonormal.

Alternative parametrization of $B_4(R)$ (and of $\partial B_4(R)=S_3(R)$ by setting $r=R$ and $dr=0$), called the {\bf Hopf 
parametrization}.
Let $S_\alp = \sin \alp$, $S_i = \sin \theta_i$ for $i=0,1$, and similarly abbreviate $\cos$ by $C$.

\begin{subequations}
\beqa
x_1 &=& rC_\alp C_0
\\
x_2 &=& rC_\alp S_0
\\
x_3 &=& r S_\alp C_1
\\
x_4 &=& r S_\alp S_1
\eeqa
\label{eqs-param-hopf-tree}
\end{subequations}
where $r\in (0, R]$ and
$\alp, \theta_0, \theta_1\in [0, \pi]$.

It's easy  to see that for this  parametrization 
\beq
(x_1)^2 + (x_2)^2 + (x_3)^2 + (x_4)
^2 = r^2
\eeq
After calculating the differentials of the $dx_i$ and adding their 
squares, cross-terms cancel and we arrive at

\beq
(ds)^2 = (dr)^2 + (r d\alp)^2+
(r C_\alp d\beta_0)^2
+ (r S_\alp d\beta_1)^2
\eeq
