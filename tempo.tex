{\bf Contact Geometry} (CG) deals with 1-forms that violate \qt{maximally} Frobenius Theorem (see Section \ref{sec-Frob-theorem}).
We begin our discussion of
CG by revisiting the shear flow
example considered in the Frobenius Theorem section.



{\bf Shear flow} example: 

$M=\RR^3$.
At each point $\vecr=(x,y,z)\in M$,
the tangent plane is a 2 dim plane


\beq
\pform{\omega}{}=
x\ul{dy} + \ul{dz}
\label{eq-shear-fear}
\eeq

Define the {\bf  distributions }
\beq
\cald_\pi=\{\vecV_\pi
\in T_\pi(M)\mid \ul{\omega}_\pi(\vecV_\pi)=0\}
\label{eq-distri1}=\ker (\ul{\omega}_\pi)
\eeq

\beq
\cald = \bigsqcup_{\pi \in M}
\cald_\pi
\eeq
 $\cald_\pi$ specifies a tangent plane at $\pi\in M$
and $\cald$ specifies a family of tangent planes.

In general, $\ul{\omega}_\pi=0$ 
constitutes on equation out of 
$n$ unknowns.
Therefore, the number of
degrees of freedom (dofs)
in $\cald_\pi$ is


\beq
\#dofs(\cald_\pi)=
\dim\cald_\pi=\dim \ker\ul{\omega_\pi}=
2\nn+1-1= 2\nn
\eeq

\begin{claim}

For the shear 1-form Eq.(\ref{eq-shear-fear})

\beq
\cald_\pi = \ker \ul{\omega}_\pi=
span
\left(
\vecpartial_1, \vecpartial_2 -x\vecpartial_3
\right)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

First note that
$\nn=1$ and $n=3$
so $\#dofs (\cald_\pi)=2$.

Set

\beq
\vecV_\pi=
V^i\vecpartial_i
\eeq
Then

\beq
\ul{\omega}_\pi(\vecV_\pi)=
xV^2 + V^3=0
\eeq
so

\beq
-xV^2= V^3
\eeq

\beq
\vecV=V^1\vecpartial_1 + V^2(\vecpartial_2 -x\vecpartial_3)
\eeq




\qed

The question
which Frobenius Theorem 
answers with necessary and sufficient
conditions is: when can the family of tangent planes $\cald$ 
be consider the
family of tangent planes to a surface.
If it can be, then that surface can be described by an equation of the type

\beq
f(x,y,z)=0
\eeq
and we say that $\cald$ is {\bf integrable}.


\begin{claim}
The shear flow example Eq.(\ref{eq-shear-fear}) is {\bf not integrable}.
\end{claim}
\proof


\beq
\xymatrix@C=4pc{
y
\\
D=(0,1, 0)\ar@{-->}[d]
_{dz=0}\ar[u]
&
C=(1, 1, 0)\ar@{-->}[l]_{dz=0}
\\
A=(0,0, 0)\ar@{-->}[r]_{dz=0}
&
B=(1,0, 0)\ar@{-->}[u]_{dz=-1}
\ar[r]
&x
}
\eeq
From

\beq
\frac{dz}{dy}=-x
\eeq
we get

\begin{enumerate}
\item $A\rarrow B$:
$dz=0$ because $dy=0$

\item $B\rarrow C$:
$dz=-1$ because $dy=1, x=1$

\item $C\rarrow D$:
$dz=0$ because $dy=0$

\item $D\rarrow A$:
$dz=0$ because $x=0$




\end{enumerate}

$dz=-1$ for full trip $A\rarrow B\rarrow C\rarrow D\rarrow A$.
Since on this closed trip,
the $z$ does not
return to its initial value,
$\cald$ cannot possibly be tangential to a family of surfaces.
\qed


Let $M$ br an $n=2\nn+1$ dimensional manifold.
A {\bf contact dif-form} is a 1-form $\pform{\omega}{}$
such that

\beq
\ul{\omega}\wedge
(\wedge \ul{d\omega})^\nn\neq 0
\label{eq-def-contact-form}
\eeq
everywhere (i.e., 
for all points in its domain).

Eq.(\ref{eq-def-contact-form})
is sufficient
but not necessary condition for
non- integrability.

Note that for any contact dif-form
$\ul{\omega}$, we have  $\ul{d\omega}\neq 0$.

If Eq.(\ref{eq-def-contact-form}) were an outright equation, it would remove one out of the $n=2\nn = 1$ dofs in $M$, but 
it's not even an equation.
As one might expect from a weak constraint  
like Eq.(\ref{eq-def-contact-form}), contact dif-forms are 
common.

Consider the cases of $\nn=1$ and $\nn>1$.


\begin{itemize}

\item CASE: $\nn=1, n=3$. 

\beq
\ul{\omega}\wedge \ul{d\omega}
\neq 0
\eeq

\begin{claim}

The shear 1-form Eq.(\ref{eq-shear-fear})
is a contact dif-form.

\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\ul{d\omega}
&=&
d(x\ul{dy} +\ul{dz})
\\
&=&
\ul{dx}\wedge \ul{dy}
\eeqa

\beqa
\ul{\omega}\wedge
\ul{d\omega} &=&
[x\ul{dy} + \ul{dz}]\wedge\ul{dx}\wedge \ul{dy}\\
&=&
\ul{dx}\wedge\ul{dy}\wedge {dz}
\\
&=&\ul{d^3x} \neq 0
\eeqa
\qed



\item CASE: $\nn
>1$, $n=2\nn+1>3$



\end{itemize}

Suppose $M\subset \RR^{2\nn+1}$ with coordinates 

\beq
(q^1, \ldots, q^\nn, p_1,\ldots, p_\nn, z)
\eeq
The {\bf standard contact dif-form}
is defined as

\beq
\pform{\omega}{}=
\ul{dz}
-\sum_i p_i \ul{dq^i}
\eeq
For this 1-form,

\beq
\ul{d\omega}=
-
\sum_i
\ul{dp_i}\wedge \ul{dq^i}
=
\sum_i
\ul{dq^i}\wedge \ul{dp_i}
\eeq
so

\beq
\ul{\omega}
\wedge
[\wedge
\ul{d\omega}
]^\nn=
\pm
\ul{dq^1}
\wedge\cdots\wedge
\ul{dq^\nn}
\wedge
\ul{dp_1}
\wedge\cdots\wedge
\ul{dp_\nn}
\neq 0
\eeq
