\subsection{M\"{o}bius Inversion Theorem}

There is a very common version of the MIT used
in Number Theory. This particular special case of MIT is its application to a 
binary lattice.


\begin{claim}
(M\"{o}bius Inversion Theorem)

If 

\beq
f(A)= \sum_B \indi(B\subset A)
\theta(B)
\eeq
then

\beq
\theta(B)=
\sum_C \indi(C\subset B)
(-1)^{|B|-|C|}
f(C)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

Let's define $f^*(A)$ as follows and prove that
$f^*(A)=f(A)$.

\begin{align}
f^*(A)&=
\sum_{B}
\indi(B\subset A)
\sum_C \indi(C\subset B)
(-1)^{|B|-|C|}
f(C)
\\
&=
\sum_{C}
\indi(C\subset A) f(C)
\underbrace{\sum_B
\indi(C\subset B \subset A) (-1)^{|B|-|C|}}_{\Delta(A, C)}
\quad\text{(changed summation order)}
\label{eq-mid-mobius-inv}
\end{align} 
$\Delta(A, C)$ seems independent of $A$. However, for the edge case $A=B=C$, it equals 1. On the other hand,
if $A\neq C$, then

\beq
\Delta(A, C)=
\sum_{|C|=0}^{|B|}
{ |B| \choose |C|}
(-1)^{|B|-|C|}
=
(1-1)^{|B|}=0
\eeq
Thus

\beq
\Delta(A, C)= 
\indi(A=C)
\eeq
and Eq.(\ref{eq-mid-mobius-inv}) reduces to
\beq
f^*(A)= \sum_C \indi(C\subset A )f(C)\indi(A=C)= f(A)
\eeq


\qed

Example:

Let 

\beq 
f(\{a,b,c, \dots\})= f_{abc\ldots}
\eeq
Consider subsets of $\Omega =\{1,2\}$

\beq
\begin{pmatrix}
f_\varnothing
\end{pmatrix}
\eeq
