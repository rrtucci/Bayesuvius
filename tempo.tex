K\"{a}hler manifold 
is defined as a $2\nn$ dimensional manifold
$M=\{(x^k, y^k)\}_{k=1}^\nn$ with 3 
gadgets

\begin{enumerate}
\item
Symplectic Structure
given by  metric $\metric(\vecX, \vecY)$

\item 
Complex Structure given bt a {\bf $\pi/2$ rotation operator} $\cala: T_\pi(M)\rarrow T_\pi(M)$
that satisfies

\beq
\cala^2=-1
\eeq
$\cala$ must also be integrable which means that locally, there must be a coordinate basis for the tangent space such that

\beq
\cala\vecpder{x^k}=\vecpder{y^k},
\quad
\cala \vecpder{y^k}=-\;\vecpder{x^k}
\eeq
for $k=1, 2, \ldots, \nn$


\item
Symplectic Structure given by a
simplectic 2-form $\ul{\s}(\vecX, \vecY)$ that satisfies

\beq
\ul{\s}(\vecX, \vecY)=
\metric(\cala\vecX, \vecY)
,\quad
\ul{d\s}=0
\eeq
Note that this implies

\beq
\metric(\cala\vecX, \cala\vecY)=
\ul{\s}(\vecX, \cala\vecY)
=
-\ul{\s}(\cala \vecY, \vecX)=-\metric(\cala^2\vecX, \vecY)=\metric(\vecX, \vecY)
\eeq
\end{enumerate}

\hrule
$M=\RR^{2\nn}$ 
with an Euclidean metric
can be turned into a K\"{a}hler manifold

\beq
d\vecr = dx^k \vecpder{x^k}
+
dy^k
\vecpder{y^k}
\eeq

metric
\beq
(ds)^2=
\sum_k
\left[
(\ul{dx^k})^2
+
(\ul{dy^k})^2
\right]
\eeq

\beq
\cala \vecpder{x^k}=
\vecpder{y^k}
,\quad
\cala
\vecpder{y^k}=-
\vecpder{x^k}
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}(
\vecpartialx{j},
\vecpartialx{k}
)=
\metric(
\vecpartialy{j},
\vecpartialx{k}
)=0
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}(
\vecpartialy{j},
\vecpartialy{k}
)=-
\metric(
\vecpartialx{j},
\vecpartialy{k}
)=0
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}(
\vecpartialx{j},
\vecpartialy{k}
)=
\metric(
\vecpartialy{j},
\vecpartialy{k}
)=
\delta_j^k
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}(
\vecpartialy{j},
\vecpartialx{k}
)=
-\metric(
\vecpartialx{j},
\vecpartialx{k}
)=-\delta_j^k
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}=
\sum_i
\ul{dx^i}
\wedge
\ul{dy^i}
\eeq

$\ul{d\s}=0$ as expected for a K\"{a}hler manifold.
In general, any 
even dimensional space  with a \Rien metric is a K\"{a}hler manifold.

General Relativity,
with its
pseudo-\Rien metric with signature $N_-=1, N_+=3$,
is not 
a K\"{a}hler manifold, because,
as can be proven,
no symplectic 2-form exists on it. Intuitively,
its impossible to divide
the GR manifold into two equivalent half spaces, because the metric in one half space will have one negative
and one positive eigenvalue whereas the metric
of the other half space
will have two positive eigenvalue.)

Complexification of K\"{a}hler manifold

Define 

\beq
z^{k\pm}=x^k \pm i y^k
\eeq

\beq
z^{k+}=
z^k, \quad z^{k-}=
\ol{z^k}
\eeq

\beq
\vecpartial_{z^{k\pm}}=
\frac{1}{2}\left(
\vecpartial_{x^k}
\mp i
\vecpartial_{y^k}
\right)
\eeq

Note that

\beq
\vecpartialz{k+}z^{k+}=
\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{x^k}
- i
\partial_{y^k})(x^k + i y^k) =1
\eeq
In general,

\beq
\vecpartialz{k\pm}z^{k\pm}
=1,
\quad 
\vecpartialz{k\pm}z^{k\mp}
=0
\eeq

\begin{claim}
$\vecpartialz{k\pm}$
are the eigenvectors of $\cala$ with eigenvalues $\pm i$.

\beq \cala \vecpartialz{k\pm}=
\pm i
\vecpartialz{k\pm}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\cala \vecpartialz{k\pm}
&=&
\cala 
\frac{1}{2}\left(
\vecpartial_{x^k}
\mp i
\vecpartial_{y^k}
\right)
\\
&=&
\frac{1}{2}\left(
\vecpartial_{y^k}
\pm i
\vecpartial_{x^k}
\right)
\\&=&
\pm i
\frac{1}{2}\left(
\mp i\vecpartial_{y^k}
+
\vecpartial_{x^k}
\right)
\\
&=&\pm i
\vecpartialz{k\pm}
\eeqa

\qed


The most general tensor $\TT$
is said to be of type $\TT{u^+ u^-\choose d^+ d^-}$ if

\beq
\TT =
T\indices{^{\alp^+, \alp^-}_
{\beta^+,\beta-}}
\vecpartialz{\alp+}
\otimes\vecpartialz{\alp-}
\otimes \ul{dz^{\beta+}}
\otimes
\otimes \ul{dz^{\beta-}}
\eeq
where

\beq
\alp^\pm = \alp_1, \alp_2, \ldots, \alp_{u^\pm}
\quad
\beta^\pm =
\beta_1, \beta_2, \dots, \beta_{d^\pm}
\eeq

Hence

\beq
\vecpartialz{k+} \in \TT{10\choose 00}\eqdef \TT^+
,\quad
\vecpartialz{k-} \in \TT{01\choose 00}\eqdef \TT^-
\eeq
and

\beq
\ul{dz^{k+}} \in \TT{00\choose 10}\eqdef \TT_{+}
,\quad
\ul{dz^{k-}} \in \TT{00\choose 01}\eqdef \TT_{-}
\eeq

As we shall see, many operations preserve the type $\TT{u^+ u^-\choose d^+ d^-}$ of the  tensor.



Note that since $\metric(\vecX, \vecY)$ is Hermitian in the real $T_\pi(M)$ basis, it must be Hermitian in the 
complex basis too, and therefore, in the complex basis

\beq
\underbrace{\metric(\vecpartialz{i+}, \vecpartialz{j+})}_
{\metric_{i+, j+}}
=
\underbrace{\metric(\vecpartialz{i-}, \vecpartialz{j-})}
_{\metric_{i-, j-}}
=0
\eeq
for all $i, j$.
Thus, if we divide the metric matrix into
4 square quadrants,
the two diagonal quadrants are zero. 

Likewise, the symplectic 
2-form only has
mixed sign components. Indeed,

\beq
\ul{\s}(\vecpartialz{i+},
\vecpartialz{j+})=
\metric(\cala\vecpartialz{i+}, \vecpartialz{j+})=i
\metric(\vecpartialz{i-},
\vecpartialz{j+})=0
\eeq
As for its same sign components,
'
\beq
\ul{\s}(\vecpartialz{i+},
\vecpartialz{j-})=
\metric(\cala\vecpartialz{i+}, \vecpartialz{j+})=i
\metric(\vecpartialz{i-},
\vecpartialz{j+})=
i\metric_{i-, j+}
\eeq

\beq
dz^k = dx^k + i dy^k
\eeq

\beq
dx^k= \frac{1}{2}(dz^{k+}
+ dz^{k-}),\quad
dy^k=
 \frac{1}{2i}(dz^{k+}
- dz^{k-})
\eeq

\beqa
\ul{\s} &=&
\sum_k \ul{dx^k}\wedge
\ul{dy^k}
\\
&=&
\frac{1}{4i}
(\ul{dz^{k+}}
+ \ul{dz^{k-}})
\wedge
(\ul{dz^{k+}}
- \ul{dz^{k-}})
\\
&=&
\frac{i}{2}
\ul{dz^{k+}}\wedge
\ul{dz^{k-}}
\eeqa







\begin{claim}

\beq
0=\nnabla \cala = \nnabla \metric =\nabla \ul{\s}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

$
\nnabla \metric=0$
follows from 
assuming the Levi-Civita connection.

One can show that 
$\nnabla \cala=0$
using the Koszul Identity (see Claim \ref{cl-Koszul})

\beq
\nnabla_\vecV \ul{\s}(\vecX, \vecY)=
\nnabla_\vecV\metric(\cala \vecX, \vecY)=0
\eeq
because both $\nnabla_\vecV\metric$
and $\nnabla_\vecV\cala$
are zero.


\qed

Suppose 
\beq
\nnabla_{\dot{\vgamma}}\vecX=0
\eeq
Then

\beq
\nnabla_{\dot{\vgamma}}(\cala \vecX)= \cala\nnabla_{\dot{\vgamma}}(\vecX)=0
\eeq

Examples

\begin{enumerate}

\item 
\begin{claim}

$S^2$, the surface of a sphere, can be turned into a 
K\"{a}hler manifold.
\end{claim}
\proof
$CP^\nn$
can be turned into a K\"{a}hler manifold.
Next we consider $\nn=1$
and show that $CP^1\cong S^2$

\beq
(ds)^2 =
\left(
\ul{R\sin\phi d\theta}
\right)^2
+ \left(
\ul{R d\phi}\right)^2
= (\ul{\chi^\theta})^2 +
(\ul{\chi^\phi})^2
\eeq

\beq
\cala \vece_\theta = \vece_\phi,\quad
\cala\vece_\phi=-\vece_\theta
\eeq


\beq
\ul{\s}=
\ul{\chi^\theta}
\wedge
\ul{\chi^\phi}=
R^2 \sin \phi\;
\ul{d\theta}
\wedge
\ul{d\phi}
\eeq

\beq
\ul{d\s}=0
\eeq

Let 

\beq
q=\theta,
\quad 
p=-R^2\cos\phi
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\s}=
\ul{dq}
\wedge 
\ul{dp}
\eeq
Confirms Darboux theorem.
Locally,
the symplectic sphere
surface $(S^2, \ul{\s})$
is isomorphic to 
the symplectic plane $(\RR^2, \ul{dq}\wedge\ul{dp})$.
But the metrics are not the same
when expressed in terms of the same variables$q, p$.

For the symplectic plane

\beq
(ds)^2=
(\ul{dq})^2
+
(\ul{dp})^2
\eeq
For the symplectic sphere
surface,

\beqa
(ds)^2&=&
(
R\sin\phi
)^2
(\ul{dq})^2
+
\frac{1}
{(R\sin\phi)^2}
(\ul{dq})^2
\\
&=&
R^2\left(1-\frac{p^2}{R^4}\right)
(\ul{dq})^2
+
\frac{1}
{R^2\left(1-\frac{p^2}{R^4}\right)}
(\ul{dp})^2
\eeqa
\qed

\item
\begin{claim}
Using the stereographic
projection, one can prove  that

\beq
S^2 \cong \CC \cong CP^1
\eeq

are all isomorphic K\"{a}hler
manifolds.
\end{claim}
\proof



First, let's review what is meant by the stereographic projection of the sphere
onto complex plane.

We shall follow the 
Wikipedia article on Stereographic projection and use lower case 
letters for the coordinates of the sphere and upper case letters for the coordinates of the point on the plane.
From Wikipedia *****


Rectangular
coordinates to  to rectangular
coordinates (See Fig.\ref{fig-stereo})
\beq
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
P_-: (X, Y)=
\frac{R}{R-z}(x, y)
\\
P_\circ: (x, y, z)=\frac{R}{R^2+ X^2+Y^2}
(2RX, 2RY, -R^2+ X^2 + Y^2)
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq


Spherical coordinates  to Polar coordinates and vice versa
\beq
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
P_-: (\rho, \Theta)=
\left(
\frac{R\sin \phi}{1-\cos\phi}, \theta
\right)
=
\left(R\cot \frac{\phi}{2}, \theta
\right)
\\
P_\circ: (\phi, \theta)=
\left(
2 \arctan \frac{R}{\rho},
\Theta
\right)
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq

Let 

\beq
Z^{\pm} = X \pm i Y
\eeq
Then


\beqa
(ds)^2 &=&
\left(
{R\sin\phi d\theta}
\right)^2
+ \left(
{R d\phi}\right)^2
\\
&=&
\frac{4R^2}{(1+|Z|^2)}
dZ^+ dZ^-
\eeqa
Fubini-Study metric

\beq
dZ^+ dZ^-= |dZ|^2=  (dX)^2 + (dY)^2
\eeq

\beqa
\ul{\s}
&=&
R^2 \sin \phi\;
\ul{d\theta}
\wedge
\ul{d\phi}
\\
&=&
\frac{2iR^2}{(1+|Z|^2)^2}
\ul{dZ^+}
\wedge
\ul{dZ^-}
\eeqa


\qed

\end{enumerate}
