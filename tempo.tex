Frobenius Thorem Section \ref{sec-Frob-theorem}


{\bf Shear flow} example

$M=\RR^3$.
At each point $\vecr=(x,y,z)\in M$,
the tangent plane is a 2 dim plane


\beq
\pform{\omega}{}=
x\ul{dy} + \ul{dz}
\label{eq-shear-fear}
\eeq

Define the {\bf  distributions }
\beq
\cald_\pi=\{\vecV
\in T_\pi(M)\mid \ul{\omega}_\pi(\vecV)=0\}
\eeq

\beq
\cald = \bigsqcup_{\pi \in M}
\cald_\pi
\eeq

 
 $\cald_\pi$ specifies a tangent plane at $\pi\in M$
and $\cald$ specifies a family of tangent planes. 
The question
which Frobenius Theorem 
answers with necessary and sufficient
conditions is: when can the family of tangent planes $\cald$ 
be consider the
family of tangent planes to a surface.
If it can be, then that surface would be described by an equation of the type

\beq
F(x,y,z)=0
\eeq
and we say that $\cald$ is {\bf integrable}.

The shear flow example is {\bf not integrable}. Indeed, suppose 
\beq
\xymatrix@C=4pc{
y
\\
D=(0,1, 0)\ar@{-->}[d]
_{dz=0}\ar[u]
&
C=(1, 1, 0)\ar@{-->}[l]_{dz=0}
\\
A=(0,0, 0)\ar@{-->}[r]_{dz=0}
&
B=(1,0, 0)\ar@{-->}[u]_{dz=-1}
\ar[r]
&x
}
\eeq
From

\beq
\frac{dz}{dy}=-x
\eeq
we get

\begin{enumerate}
\item $A\rarrow B$:
$dz=0$ because $dy=0$

\item $B\rarrow C$:
$dz=-1$ because $dy=1, x=1$

\item $C\rarrow D$:
$dz=0$ because $dy=0$

\item $D\rarrow A$:
$dz=0$ because $x=0$

$dz=-1$ for full trip $A\rarrow B\rarrow C\rarrow D\rarrow A$.
Since on this closed trip,
the $z$ does not
return to itrs initial value,
$\cald$ cannot possibly be tangential to a family of surfaces.

\end{enumerate}


Let $M$ br an $n=2\nn+1$ dimensional manifold.
A {bf contact form} is a 1-form $\pform{\omega}{}$
such that

\beq
\ul{\omega}\wedge
(\wedge \ul{d\omega})^\nn=0
\eeq

\begin{itemize}

\item $\nn=1, n=3$. 

\beq
\ul{\omega}\wedge \ul{d\omega}=0
\eeq

\begin{claim}

The shear 1-form Eq.(\ref{eq-shear-fear})
is a contact form

\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\ul{d\omega}
&=&
d(x\ul{dy} +\ul{dz})
\\
&=&
\ul{dx}\wedge \ul{dy}
\eeqa

\beqa
\ul{\omega}\wedge
\ul{d\omega} &=&
[x\ul{dy} + \ul{dz}]\wedge\ul{dx}\wedge \ul{dy}\\
&=&
\ul{dx}\wedge\ul{dy}\wedge {dz}
\\
&=&\ul{d^3x} \neq 0
\eeqa
\qed

\begin{claim}

For the shear 1-form Eq.(\ref{eq-shear-fear})

\beq
\cald = \ker \ul{\omega}=
span
\left(
\vecpartial_3, \vecpartial_2 -x\vecpartial_z
\right)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

\item arbitrary $\nn
$

\end{itemize}
