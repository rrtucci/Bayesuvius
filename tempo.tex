\subsection{Lie Derivatives}


\subsubsection{LD from Flow}


The Lie derivative
measures how a quantity (a tensor 
of arbitrary type)
changes along a
streamline of a vector field.


The {\bf Lie Derivative $\call_\vecX$  of a vector field
$\vecV$} equals
(proof given below)

\beq
\call_\vecX\vecV= [\vecX, \vecV]\in\gerX(M)
\eeq

\beq
\begin{array}{cc}
\bcen
\xymatrix{
& \ar[dl]|{\oper{\vecV}}
\\
\call_\vecX \vecV(\vecr)
&&\vecr
\ar[ul]|{\oper{\vecX}}
\ar[dl]|{\oper{\vecV}}
\\
&\ar[ul]|{-\oper{\vecX}}
}\ecen
&
\call_{\vecX}\vecV(\vecr)=
\left[
\oper{\vecX}, \oper{\vecV}
\right](\vecr)\in \RR
\end{array}
\eeq

Meaning of Lie Bracket:

Suppose

\beq
\oper{\vecX} = X^i\partial_i=
\vecX\cdot \nabla,
\quad
\oper{\vecV} =  V^j \partial_j = \vecV\cdot \nabla
\eeq

\beqa
\call_\vecX(\vecV)
&=&
\vecX\cdot \nabla
[
\vecV\cdot \nabla \vecr
]
-
\vecV\cdot \nabla
[
\vecX\cdot \nabla \vecr
]
\\
&=&
[\vecX\cdot \nabla (\vecV)-
\vecV\cdot \nabla (\vecX )
]\cdot
\nabla \vecr
\\
&=&
[\vecX\cdot \nabla (U^i)-
\vecV\cdot \nabla (X^i )
]
\underbrace{\partial_i \vecr}_{\vecpder{x^i}}
\\
&=&
\oper{\vecX}(\vecV)-
\oper{\vecV}(\vecX)
\eeqa

\begin{claim}

\beq
\call_\vecX\vecV =
[\vecX, \vecV]
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

Consider a flow\footnote{Flows are discussed in Section \ref{sec-integral-curves}} $\vPhi^t: M\rarrow M$ defined by


\beq
\dot{\vPhi}^t = \vecX_{\vPhi^t}
\eeq
or, informally,

\beq
\vPhi^t = \invpder{t}\vecX_{\vPhi^t}
\eeq

By definition


\beq
\call_\vecX V_\pi
=
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0}
\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
\indi_{\vPhi^t}\vecV_\pi
\eeq
where

\beq
\indi_{\vPhi^t}:
T_\pi(M)\rarrow T_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}(M),
\quad
\indi_{\vPhi^t}\vecV_\pi=
\vecV_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
\eeq
and 

\beq
\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)}:
 T_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}(M)
\rarrow T_\pi(M),
\quad 
\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)} \vecV_\pi
\mapsto
\vecU_\pi
\eeq
Note that $\vecU_\pi\neq \vecV_\pi$.
Otherwise,
$\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)}$ would be the
inverse of
$\indi_{\vPhi^t}$
and $\call_\vecX \vecV$ would be zero. 

Let
\beq
\vecV = V^i\vecpartial_i,
\quad
\vecX = X^i\vecpartial_i
\eeq
Then

\beqa
\call_\vecX \vecV_\pi
&=&
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0}
\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
\left(\vecV_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}\right)
\\
&=&
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0}
\dvPhi{-t}{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
\left(V^i_{\vPhi^t(\pi)
}\vecpartial_i\right)
\\
&=&
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0} \ul{dx^j}
\left[\AT{\partial_j
\vPhi^{-t}}
{\vPhi^t(\pi)}\right]
\left(V^i_{\vPhi^t(\pi)
}\vecpartial_i\right)
\\
&=&
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0} \AT{\partial_j
\vPhi^{-t}}
{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
\underbrace
{V^j_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}}_{\indi_{\vPhi^t}
V^j_\pi}
\eeqa
Hence

\beq
\call_\vecX V^i_\pi=
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0}
{A\indices{^i_j}(t)B^j(t)}
\eeq
where

\beq
\frac{dA\indices{^i_j}(0)}{dt}=
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}
{t=0} \AT{\partial_j
\Phi^{-t, i}}
{\vPhi^t(\pi)}
=-\partial_jX^i_\pi
\eeq

\beq
A\indices{^i_j}(0)
=\partial_j \pi^i
=
\pder{x^i}{x^j}=
\delta^i_j
\eeq


\beq
\frac{dB^j(0)}{dt}=
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}{t=0}
\underbrace{V^j_{\vPhi^t(\pi)}}
_{\indi_{\vPhi^t}
V^j_\pi}
=
\dot{\Phi}^k\partial_kV^j_\pi=
X^k_\pi\partial_k V^j_\pi
\eeq


\beq
B^j(0)= V^j_\pi
\eeq
Thus

\beqa
[\call_\vecX V_\pi]^i
&=&
\frac{d A\indices{^i_j}(0)}
{dt} B^j(0)
+
A\indices{^i_j}(0)
\frac{dB^j(0)}{dt}
\\
&=&
\left[
-\partial_j X_\pi^i
\right]V^j_\pi
+
\delta^i_j
\left[X^k_\pi \partial_k \vecV_\pi^j\right]
\\
&=&
-V^j_\pi\partial_j X_\pi^i
+
X^k_\pi \partial_k V_\pi^i
\\
&=&
[\vecX, \vecV]_\pi^i
\eeqa
\qed



The {\bf Lie derivative of a dif-form}
of any degree $p$
(including $p=0$)
is given by
{\bf Cartan's Magic Formula}:

\beq
\begin{array}{cc}
\bcen
\xymatrix{
&\ar[dl]|{d}
\\
\call_\vecX \pform{\omega}{p}
&& \pform{\omega}{p}
\ar[ul]|{i_\vecX}
\ar[dl]|d
\\
&\ar[ul]|{i_\vecX}
}\ecen
&
\boxed
{\call_\vecX = i_\vecX d + d\;i_\vecX}
\end{array}
\eeq
Note that in Cartan's Magic Formula,  $d$ is independent
of $\vecX$
whereas $i_\vecX$ does depend on $\vecX$.
$d$ raises the degree of a dif-form by 1 and $i_\vecX$
lowers it by one,
so both summands in Cartan Magic Formula
leave the degree
of a dif-form
unchanged.\footnote{A good mnemonic is  $d\rarrow$ differentiate 
and $i_\vecX\rarrow$ integrate.}

\begin{itemize}
\item 0-forms:

\beqa
\call_{\vecX}f(\cdot)
&=&
i_\vecX(df) + \cancel{d(i_\vecX f)}^0
\\
&=&
\ul{df}(\vecX)
\\
&=&
\vecX\cdot \nabla f
=  \oper{\vecX}(f)
\eeqa

\item 1-form:

Suppose if
$\omega$
and $\vecX$ 
are defined
as follows:

\beq
\ul{\omega}=
\sum_{i=1}^n
\omega_i(x)\ul{dx^i}
\eeq
and

\beq
\vecX =\sum_i X^i(x) \vecpder{x^i}
\eeq

\beq
i_\vecX\ul{\omega}=
\ul{\omega}(\vecX)=
\omega_i X^i
\eeq

\beq
\boxed
{d(i_\vecX \ul{\omega})=
\partial_j
(\omega_i X^i)
\ul{dx^j}}
\label{eq-cmf-part1}
\eeq

\beqa
d\ul{\omega}
&=&
d \left[ \omega_i
\ul {dx^i}\right]
\\
&=&
\partial_j{\omega_i}
\ul{dx^j}\wedge
\ul{dx^i}
\\
&=&
\frac{1}{2}
[\partial_j \omega_i -\partial_i\omega_j]
\ul{dx^j}\wedge
\ul{dx^i}
\eeqa

\beq
i_\vecX
[\ul{dx^j}
\wedge \ul{dx^i}]=
X^j \ul{dx^i}-
X^i \ul{dx^j}
\eeq

\beq
\boxed
{i_\vecX
d\ul{\omega}=
\partial_j\omega_i
\left[
X^j \ul{dx^i}-
X^i\ul{dx^j}
\right]}
\label{eq-cmf-part2}
\eeq

Combining Eq.(\ref{eq-cmf-part1}) and Eq.(\ref{eq-cmf-part2}), we finally get

\beqa
\call_\vecX \pform{\omega}{}
&=&
\left(
X^j\partial_j
\omega_k
+\omega_j \partial_k X^j
\right)
\pform{dx^k}{}
\\
&=&
\vecX\cdot\nabla \pform{\omega}{}
-
\omega_j
\pform{d X^j}{}
\eeqa

\end{itemize}


\subsubsection{LD from Lie Brackets }\label{sec-Lie-der-tensor}

We started by 
showing that the Lie derivative of a vector field (type $1\choose 0$ tensor)
is just a Lie bracket.
Then we introduced 
Cartan's magic formula
which allows us to calculate
the Lie derivative
of a general dif-form
(type $0\choose d$ tensor).
In this section,
we will show how to
calculate te Lie derivative
of a general tensor
of type $u\choose d$.


Note that the expressions for Lie derivatives  given
in this section are free of
any reference to the 
following
\begin{itemize} \item a connection $\nnabla$
\item a metric $\metric$ 
\item coordinates and their
derivatives $\partial_\mu$.
\end{itemize}
They only avail themselves of the action of vector fields and Lie brackets.


\begin{claim}
Suppose $f$ is a
scalar function
(i.e., ${0\choose 0}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX f = \vecX(f)=
X^i\partial_i f
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

\begin{claim}
Suppose $\vecY$ is a
vector field
(i.e., ${1\choose 0}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX \vecY = 
[\vecX, \vecY]
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed


\begin{claim}
Suppose $\pform{\omega}{}$ is a 1-form (i.e., covector, ${0\choose 1}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX
\ul{\omega}(\vecY)=
\vecX(\ul{\omega}(\vecY))-
\ul{\omega}(
[\vecX, \vecY]
)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed




\begin{claim}
For any smooth tensors
$S$ and $T$,
\beq
\call_\vecX(S\otimes T)=
(\call_\vecX S)\otimes
T
+
S\otimes 
(\call_\vecX T)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

\begin{claim}
Suppose $T$ is a type
$u\choose d$ tensor
and

\beq
\ul{\alp}= (\ul{\alp^1},\ul{\alp^2},\cdots, \ul{\alp^u}), \quad
\vecY = (\vecY_1,{\vecY}_2,\cdots,\vecY_d)
\eeq
Then

\beq
(\call_\vecX)
T(\ul{\alp},\vecY)
=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\vecX[
T(\ul{\alp},\vecY)
]
\\
-\sum_{i=1}^u
\AT{T(\ul{\alp},\vecY)}
{\ul{\alp^i}
\rarrow \call_\vecX
\ul{\alp^i}}
\\
-\sum_{i=1}^d
\AT{T(\ul{\alp},\vecY)}
{\vecY_i
\rarrow 
[\vecX, \vecY_i]}
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq
where

\beq
(\call_\vecX\ul{\alp^i})(\vecZ)=
\vecX(\ul{\alp^i}(\vecZ))
-
\ul{\alp^i}
([\vecX, \vecZ])
\eeq


\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

For metric $\metric(\vecU, \vecV)$
(type ${0\choose 2}$
tensor) 

\beq
\call_\vecX \metric(\vecU, \vecV)
=
\vecX(\metric(\vecU, \vecV))
-
\metric([\vecX, \vecU], \vecV)
-
\metric(\vecU, [\vecX, \vecV])
\label{eq-lie-deriv-metric}
\eeq
A {\bf Killing field}
is defined as a vector field $\vecX$
along which the metric $\metric$ does 
not change:


\beq
\call_\vecX\metric =0
\eeq



\subsubsection{LD from Covariant Derivatives}

In this section, we will rewrite the connection-independent formulae
for the Lie derivative
of a general 
tensor in a less 
fundamental way that \ul{that 
assumes the LCC} (Levi-Civita connection).
In this less
fundamental
rewriting,
the Lie brackets are
replaced by covariant
derivatives. 
The rewriting is valid
for a theory with a pseudo-\Rien metric,
such as General
Relativity.

\begin{claim}
.
\beq
(\call_\vecX  \vecY)=
X^\mu \nnabla_\mu
Y^\nu -
Y^\mu \nnabla_\mu X^\nu
\eeq


\end{claim}
\proof
\beq
\call_\vecX \vecY=
[\vecX, \vecY]
\eeq

\beq
\vecX = X^\mu \vecpartial_\mu,\quad
\vecY = Y^\nu \vecpartial_\nu
\eeq

\beq
[{\vecX}, {\vecY}]
=
X^\mu(\partial_\mu
Y^\nu)\vecpartial_\nu
-
(X\swap Y)
\eeq

Next, we replace 
the partial derivatives
by covariant ones because the \Chris symbols
cancel.

\beq
\nnabla_\mu V^\nu=
\partial_\mu V^\nu
+
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu_\lam}}V^\lam
\eeq
for $V=X, Y$


\beq
X^\mu\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu\lam}}Y^\lam
-
(X\swap Y)=0
\eeq
because

\beq
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu\lam}} = 
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\lam\mu}}
\eeq
The \Chris symbols are symmetric
in $\mu, \lam$ because the torsion
is assumed to be zero.

\qed

\begin{claim}
If 
\beq
\ul{\omega}=\omega_\nu \ul{dx^\nu}
\eeq
then

\beq
(\call_\vecX  \ul{\omega})_\nu
=
X^\mu \nnabla_\mu \omega_\nu
+
\omega_\mu \nnabla_\nu X^\mu
\eeq

\end{claim}
\proof

Start from
\beq
(\call_\vecX  \ul{\omega})_\nu
=
X^\mu \partial_\mu \omega_\nu
+
\omega_\mu \partial_\nu X^\mu
\eeq
Now replace the 
partial derivatives 
by covariant derivatives.

\beq
X^\mu\nnabla_\mu \omega_\nu=
X^\mu\left[
\partial_\mu \omega_\nu -
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\mu\nu}}\omega_\lam\right]
\eeq

\beq
\omega_\mu\nnabla_\nu X^\mu=
\omega_\mu
\left[\partial_\nu X^\mu +
\Gamma\indices{^\mu_{\nu\lam}}X^\lam\right]
\eeq


\beq
-X^\mu \Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\mu\nu}}\omega_\lam
+
\underbrace{\omega_\mu
\Gamma\indices{^\mu_{\nu\lam}}X^\lam}_{
\omega_\lam
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\mu}}X^\mu
}
=0
\eeq
\qed

\begin{claim}
If $T$ is a type
$u\choose d$ tensor
and

\beq
\mu = (\mu_1\mu_2\cdots \mu_u), \quad
\nu = (\nu_1\mu_2\cdots\nu_d)
\eeq

\beq
T= T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}
\vecpartial_{\mu_1}\otimes
\vecpartial_{\mu_2}\otimes
\cdots\otimes
\vecpartial_{\mu_u}
\otimes
\ul{dx}^{\nu_1}\otimes
\ul{dx}^{\nu_1}\otimes
\cdots\otimes
\ul{dx}^{\nu_d}
\eeq
then

\beq
(\call_\vecX T)
\indices{^\mu_\nu}=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
X^\lam
\nnabla_\lam
T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}
\\
-
\sum_{i=1}^u
\AT{T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}}{\mu_i\rarrow \lam}
\nnabla_\lam X^{\mu_i}
\\
+ 
\sum_{i=1}^d
\AT{T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}}{\nu_i\rarrow \lam}
\nnabla_{\nu_i} X^{\lam}
\end{array}
\right.
\label{eq-lie-tt}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

Note  that the first term 
on the right side of  Eq.(\ref{eq-lie-tt}) is

\beq
X^\lam
\nnabla_\lam
T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}=
\underbrace{X^\lam\partial_\lam}_{\oper{\vecX}}
 T\indices{^\mu_\nu}  +  \text{\Chris term}
\eeq

For the LLC, the Lie derivative 
of the metric $\metric(\vecU, \vecV)$ takes the following simple form in the coordinate basis:

\beq
[\call_\vecX 
\metric]_{\mu\nu}= \nnabla_\mu X_\nu 
+ (\mu\swap \nu)
\eeq
Hence, the Killing fields satisfy

\beq
0=
[\call_\vecX 
\metric]_{\mu\nu}= 
\nnabla_\mu X_\nu 
+ (\mu\swap \nu)
\eeq

\subsubsection{Examples}

\begin{enumerate}
\item

\begin{claim}
Suppose $M=\RR^2$ with Euclidean metric
\beq
\metric(\vecU, \vecV)=
\left[\ul{dx})^2 + (\ul{dy})^2\right
](\vecU, \vecV)= U^1V^1 + U^2V^2 =
\vecU^T \begin{bmatrix}1&0
\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\vecV
\eeq
If

\beq
\vecX = y \vecpder{x}=\begin{bmatrix}y\\0\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
then 
\beq
\call_\vecX\metric = 2\ul{dx}\ul{dy}
\eeq
$\call_\vecX\metric\neq 0$ so 
$\vecX$ is not a Killing field.
This $\vecX$ is called a {\bf shear flow}.
The metric changes along a shear flow.
\end{claim}
\proof

First proof (from pullback definition):

If

\beq
\gamma(\tau;t)=
\begin{bmatrix}x+yt\\y\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
then
\beq 
\pi=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix},
\quad
 \vecX_\gamma = \dot{\vgamma}
\eeq

\beq
\metric_\pi = (\ul{dx})^2  + (\ul{dy})^2,
\quad
\metric_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)} = (\ul{dx} + t\ul{dy})^2 
+ (\ul{dy})^2
\eeq

\beq
\call_\vecX \metric =
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}{\tau=0}\metric_{\gamma(t)}
=2 \ul{dx}\ul{dy}
\eeq

Second proof (from formula):

\beqa
(\call_\vecX\metric)_{\mu\nu}
&=&
X^\lam \partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}
+
\metric_{\lam\nu}
\partial_\mu X^\lam
+
\metric_{\mu\lam}
\partial_\nu X^\lam
\\
&=&
X^\lam \partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}
+
(\partial_\mu X_\nu + (\mu\swap \nu))
\eeqa

\beq
\partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}=0
\eeq

\beq
\vecX=
\begin{bmatrix}
y\\0
\end{bmatrix}
\implies 
[\partial_\mu X_\nu]
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1
\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
so 

\beq
[(\call_\vecX\metric)_{\mu\nu}]= 2 
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1
\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}
\eeq

\qed

\item
\begin{claim}
For a { general affine connection $\nnabla$}, in the coordinate basis
\beq
[\call_\vecX \metric]_{\mu\nu}
= 
X^\lam \nnabla_\lam\metric_{\mu\nu}+
\nnabla_\mu X_\nu+
\nnabla_\nu X_\mu
+
X^\lam\left[\TT_{\lam\mu\nu} + (\mu\swap \nu)\right]
\eeq
where $\TT_{\lam \mu\nu}$ represents
the torsion tensor with the $\lam$
index lowered appropriately.
\end{claim}
\proof

The coordinate-free
definition is
\beq
(\call_\vecX \metric)
(\vecU, \vecV)
=
\vecX[\metric(\vecU, \vecV)]
-
\metric([\vecX, \vecU], \vecV)
-
\metric(\vecU,
[\vecX, \vecV])
\eeq

\beq
\vecX[\metric(\vecU, \vecV)]
=
(\nnabla_\vecX\metric)(\vecU, \vecV)
+
\metric)(
\nnabla_\vecX
\vecU, \vecV)
+
\metric)(\vecU, 
\nnabla_\vecX
\vecV)
\eeq

\beq
[\vecX, \vecY]=
\nnabla_\vecX\vecY
-
\nnabla_\vecY \vecX 
-
\TT(\vecX, \vecY)
\eeq
for $\vecY = \vecU, \vecV$.
Substituting, we get

\beq
(\call_\vecX)
(\vecU, \vecV)
=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
(\nnabla_\vecX\metric)
(\vecU, \vecV)
\\
+
\metric
(\nnabla_\vecU
\vecX, \vecV)
+
\metric
(\vecU, 
\nnabla_\vecV
\vecX)
\\
+
\metric
(\TT(\vecX,\vecU), \vecV)
+
\metric
(\vecU, 
\TT(\vecX,\vecV))
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq
Now replace

\beq
\vecU=\vecpartial_\mu,
\quad \vecV=\vecpartial_\nu,
\quad
\vecX = X^\lam \vecpartial_\lam
\eeq


\beq
\nnabla_\vecU\vecX=
 \vecpartial_\lam\nnabla_\mu X^\lam
\eeq

\beq
\quad
\metric
(\nnabla_\vecU
\vecX, \vecV)=
\metric_{\lam\nu}
\nnabla_\mu X^\lam=
\nnabla_\mu X_\nu
\eeq

\beq
\TT(\vecX, \vecU)=
X^\lam \TT\indices{^\rho_{\lam\mu}}
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

\beq
\metric(\TT(\vecX, \vecU), \vecV)=
X^\lam \TT\indices{^\rho_{\lam\mu}}
 \metric_{\rho\nu}
=X^\lam \TT_{\nu\lam\mu}
\eeq
\qed




\end{enumerate}



Consider a manifold $M$.


Define a {\bf flow}  to be a map

\beq
\phi(\cdot;\tau):M \rarrow M,
\quad \pi\mapsto 
\phi(\pi;\tau)=\gamma(\tau;\pi)
\eeq
where $\tau\in[0,1]$  is a parameter of the function $\phi(\cdot;\tau)$.


The Lie derivative $\call_\vecX$
can be defined in terms of pullbacks
a follows. Consider the action of 
$\call_\vecX$ on the metric function
$\metric(\vecU, \vecV)$ as an example.



\beq
\call_{\vecX_\pi}
\metric_\pi(\vecU_\pi ,\vecV_\pi)
=
\left(\AT{\frac{d}{d\tau}}{\tau=0}
\pback{\pi}{\phi(\pi;\tau)}
{\metric_\pi}
\right)(\vecU_\pi ,\vecV_\pi)
=
\left[\AT{\frac{d}{d\tau}}{\tau=0}
\metric_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)}\right]
(\vecU_{\pi},
 \vecV_{\pi})
\eeq
where 
\beq
\phi(\pi;\tau=0)=\pi
\eeq

\beq
\vecX_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)}=\dot{\vgamma}(\tau;\pi)
\eeq
