\section{The 3 Languages of Differential Geometry}
\label{sec-3-lang-diff-geo}

In this section,
we discuss the 3 Languages
of Differential Geometry
and give examples of how to convert equations from one language to another.
One of those languages (the index-free one)
does not assume a
particular frame whereas
the other 2 assume
different frame types
that are already familiar to us.
Let us start by discussing
those 2 frame types
and how to transform between them. By a frame (and coframe)
we mean a t-basis  (and dual t-basis) for tangent vectors (and covectors).

\begin{enumerate} 
\item Coordinate  (\Chris) t-basis

\beq
\vecpartial_\mu=\vecpder{x^\mu}, \quad
\av{\ul{dx^\nu}|\vecpartial_\mu}=\delta_\mu^\nu
,\quad
\vecpartial_\mu\cdot
\vecpartial_\nu= \delta^\mu_\nu
\eeq

\item Cartan's (no-holonomic) t-basis

In general, this t-basis is  neither
normalized nor orthogonal.
\beq
\av{\ul{\chi^b}|\vece_a}
= \delta^b_a,
\quad
\vece_a\cdot \vece_a \neq
1,\quad
\vece_a\cdot \vece_b \neq
0 \text{ for $a\neq b$}
\eeq

We can view the coordinate 
t-basis as a special case of the
Cartan t-basis
and set

\beq
\vece_a= \vecpartial_a,\quad
\ul{\chi^a}=
\ul{dx^a}
\eeq

The t-basis used in curvilinear coordinates
can also be viewed as as a special case of the Cartan t-basis.

\beq 
\vece_a=\frac{1}{\elli{a}} \vecpartial_{\phi^a},
\quad 
\ul{\chi^a}=
\elli{a} \ul{d\phi^a}
\eeq
The curvilinear t-basis is orthogonal but 
not normalized.
\end{enumerate}

Sometimes, when transforming 
from one of the 3 languages
of Differential Geometry to another, we need to
transform from the
coordinate t-basis to the Cartan t-basis or vice versa.
Fig.\ref{fig-ops-between-bases} defines  the matrices
that accomplish these transformations.

\begin{figure}[h!]
$$
\xymatrix@C=4pc{
\ket{\vecpartial_\mu}
\ar@{-}[r]
\ar@/_1pc/[d]_{E\indices{^\mu_a}}
&
\stackrel{\bra{\ul{dx^\nu}}}{}
\ar[r]
\ar@/_1pc/[d]_{X\indices{^b_\nu}}
&\delta_\mu^\nu
\\
\ket{\vece_a}
\ar@{-}[r]
\ar@/_1pc/[u]_{E\indices{^a_\mu}}
&\stackrel{\bra{\ul{\chi^b}}}{}
\ar[r]
\ar@/_1pc/[u]_{X\indices{^\nu_b}}
&\delta^b_a
}$$
\caption{Definition
of matrices that transform between a coordinate t-basis and a Cartan t-basis. We prove that $X\indices{^a_\mu}=E\indices{^a_\mu}$ and $X\indices{^\mu_a}=E\indices{^\mu_a}$.}
\label{fig-ops-between-bases}
\end{figure}
These matrices must satisfy the following identities:

\beq
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
E\indices{^\mu_a}\ket{\vecpartial_\mu}=
\ket{\vece_a}
,\quad
E\indices{^a_\mu}\ket{\vece_a}=
\ket{\vecpartial_\mu}
\\
E\indices{^\mu_a}=
\av{\ul{dx^\mu}|\vece_a}
,\quad
E\indices{^a_\mu}=
\av{\ul{\chi^a}|\vecpartial_\mu}
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq

\beq
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
X\indices{^b_\nu}\bra{\ul{dx^\nu}}=
\bra{\ul{\chi^b}}
,\quad
X\indices{^\nu_b}\bra{\ul{\chi^b}}=
\bra{\ul{dx^\nu}}
\\
X\indices{^b_\nu}=
\av{\ul{\chi^b}|\vecpartial_\nu}=E\indices{^b_\nu},
\quad
X\indices{^\nu_b}=
\av{\ul{dx^\nu}|\vece_b}=E\indices{^\nu_b}
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq



\beq
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
X\indices{^b_\mu}E\indices{^\mu_a}
=
\delta_a^b
,\quad
X\indices{^\nu_a}E\indices{^a_\mu}
=\delta^\nu_\mu
\\
\av{\ul{\chi^b}|\vecpartial_\mu}
\av{\ul{dx^\mu}|\vece_a} = \delta_a^b
,\quad
\av{\ul{dx^\nu}|\vece_a}
\av{\ul{\chi^a}|\vecpartial_\mu}
=
\delta_\mu^\nu
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq
Note that

\beq
\ul{\chi^b}= \ul{dx^b},
\quad \vece_a = \vecpartial_a
\implies
E\indices{^\mu_a}= E\indices{^a_\mu}=\delta_a^\mu
\eeq

Note that normally, if
an equation only has 
indices referring to a
single basis, we sometimes
use lower case Latin indices $i, a$, etc., whereas other times we use lower case Greek indices $\mu, \nu$, etc.
But in this section,
some equations refer to
2 different bases at the same time, and Latin indices refer to one basis,
and Greek indices refer to a different basis.


Note that the Dirac bra-ket notation
provided
above
expresses the same
distinction that
we are already 
expressing by 
underlining 
dif-forms and
putting a vector
sign over vectors. 
Thus 
bra-ket notation
is redundant
in this case.
We are including
it here for pedagogical
purposes. It echoes
certain 
familiar properties such as
orthogonality
and completeness of a
basis. 

\beq
\text{Orthogonality: }
\av{\ul{\chi^b}|\vece_a}
= \delta^b_a
\eeq

\beq
\text{Completeness: }
\av{\ul{dx^\nu}|\vece_a}
\av{\ul{\chi^a}|\vecpartial_\mu}
=
\delta_\mu^\nu
\eeq



Note that we could replace $E\indices{^\mu_a}$
by its transpose $(E^T)
\indices{_a^\mu}$
and then its indices would be \qt{slopping upwards} instead of 
\qt{slopping downwards}.
Different authors choose
different conventions for this.
We have chosen the convention in which the
indices for both
$E\indices{^\mu_a}$
and its inverse $E\indices{^a_\mu}$
are both slopping downwards.
Likewise,
we have chosen the 
convention
in which the indices
of $X$ and its inverse are
both slopping downwards.
The two $X$ and two $E$ matrices turn
out to be the same anyway.
The important thing is that all of the equations above can be represented
in an intuitive,  unique and consistent way.

The 3 languages
of Differential Geometry
are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item
{\bf Coordinate frame (\Chris) notation ($\Gamma$N):} Everything is expressed in terms of components in a coordinate basis.

\beq
\vecpartial_\mu =\vecpder{x^\mu}
\eeq

\beq
\metric =
\metric_{\mu\nu}
\ul{dx^\mu}\otimes
\ul{dx^\nu}
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\vecpartial_\nu =
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

For a vector field
$\vecY = Y^\nu \vecpartial_\mu$


\beq
(\nnabla_\mu \vecY)^\rho=
\partial_\mu Y^\rho
+
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}Y^\nu
\eeq

\item {\bf Cartan (moving-frame)
dif-form notation (CN):} Everything is expressed in terms of dif- forms, frames, coframes, and connection 1-forms.

No $\vecpartial_a$, $\vece_a$ instead

\beq
\metric=
\metric_{ab}\ul{\chi^a}
\otimes
\ul{\chi^b}
\eeq


\beq
\metric=
\eta_{ab}
\ul{\chi^a}
\otimes
\ul{\chi^b}
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla \vece_b=
\vece_a
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}
(\vecX)
\eeq



\item {\bf Abstract, index-free notation (AN):} Everything is expressed in terms of vector fields, tensor fields, and covariant derivatives, without choosing coordinates or a frame.

\beq
\nnabla: \ger{X}(M)\times\ger{X}(M)
\rarrow
\ger{X}(M)
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla_{f\vecX}\vecY=
f\nnabla_\vecX \vecY
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla_\vecX(f\vecY)=
\vecX(f)\vecY +
f\nnabla_\vecX \vecY
\eeq

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{CN to $\Gamma$N}

\begin{claim}

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_a}\vece_b
=
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}=
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
E\indices{^c_\rho}
\ul{dx^\mu}
\eeq
If 

\beq
\ul{\chi^b}= \ul{dx^b},
\quad \vece_a = \vecpartial_a
\eeq
then

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_a}\vece_b
=\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{ab}}
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}=\Gamma\indices {^c_{\mu b}}\ul{dx^\mu}
\eeq


\end{claim}
\proof


\beqa
\nnabla_{\vece_a}\vece_b
&=&
\nnabla_{\vece_a}(
E\indices{^\nu_b}\vecpartial_\nu
)
\\
&=&
\vece_a(E\indices{^\nu_b})
\vecpartial_\nu
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\nnabla_{\vece_a}
\vecpartial_\nu
\\
&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\nu_b})\vecpartial_\nu
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\nnabla_{\vece_a}
\vecpartial_\nu
\\
&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\nu_b})\vecpartial_\nu
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\vecpartial_\nu
\\
&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\nu_b})\vecpartial_\nu
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}
\vecpartial_\rho
\\
&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
\vecpartial_\rho
\\
&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
E\indices{^c_\rho}
\vece_c
\eeqa

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_a}
(\vece_b)
=\vece_c \ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vece_a)
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vece_a)=
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
E\indices{^c_\rho}
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}=
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vece_a)\ul{\chi^a}
\eeq

\beqa
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}&=&
E\indices{^\mu_a}
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
E\indices{^c_\rho}\ul{\chi^a}
\\
&=&
\left(
\partial_\mu
(E\indices{^\rho_b})
+
E\indices{^\nu_b}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\right)
E\indices{^c_\rho}
\ul{dx^\mu}
\eeqa

\qed


\subsection{AN to $\Gamma$N}

\beq
\nnabla_\vecX \vecY
=
\underbrace{X(Y^\rho)
}_{X^\mu\partial_\mu(Y^\rho)}\vecpartial_\rho
+Y^\nu
\underbrace{\nnabla_\vecX \vecpartial_\nu}_{X^\mu\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu}}\vecpartial_\rho}
\eeq



\Rie 
tensor in all 3 languages
\beq
\boxed{\RR(\vecX, \vecY)(\vecZ)
=
\nnabla_\vecX \nnabla_\vecY
\vecZ
-
\nnabla_\vecY \nnabla_\vecX
\vecZ
-
\nnabla_{[\vecX, \vecY]}
\vecZ}
\label{eq-rie-inan}
\eeq

\beq
\RR(\vecpartial_\mu, \vecpartial_\nu)(\vecpartial_\s)
=
\RR\indices{
^\rho_{\mu\nu\s}}\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

\begin{claim}
Eq.(\ref{eq-rie-inan})
(index free definition of $\RR$)
implies

\beq
\RR
\indices{^\rho_{\mu\nu\s}}=
[\partial_\mu\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\nu\s}
}
+\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\lam}}
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}]
-(\mu\swap\nu)
\eeq

\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\RR\indices{
^\rho_{\mu\nu\s}}\vecpartial_\rho
&=&
\RR(\vecpartial_\mu, \vecpartial_\nu)\vecpartial_\s
\\
&=&
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\nu}\vecpartial_\s
-
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\nu}\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\vecpartial_\s
-
\nnabla_{[\vecpartial_\mu, \vecpartial_\nu]}
\vecpartial_\s
\eeqa

\beq
[\vecpartial_\mu,
\vecpartial_\nu]=0
\eeq

\beq
\RR\indices{
^\rho_{\mu\nu\s}}\vecpartial_\rho
=
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\nu}\vecpartial_\s
-
(\mu\swap \nu)
\eeq

\beqa
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\nu}\vecpartial_\s
&=&
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}(
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}
\vecpartial_\lam
)
\\
&=&
\partial_\mu(\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}})
\vecpartial_\lam
+\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}
\vecpartial_\lam
\\
&=&
\partial_\mu(\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}})
\vecpartial_\lam
+
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\lam}}
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}
\vecpartial_\rho
\\
&=&
\left(\partial_\mu(\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\nu\s}})
+
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\lam}}
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}
\right)
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeqa

\beq
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\nu}
\nnabla_{\vecpartial_\mu}\vecpartial_\s
=
\left(\partial_\nu(\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\s}})
+
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\mu\s}}
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\nu\lam}}
\right)
\vecpartial_\rho
\eeq

\beq
\RR\indices{
^\rho_{\mu\nu\s}}\vecpartial_\rho=
\left(\partial_\mu(\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\nu\s}})
+
\Gamma\indices{^\rho_{\mu\lam}}
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\nu\s}}
\right)
\vecpartial_\rho
-(\mu\swap \nu)
\eeq

\qed

\subsection{AN to CN}


\beq
\nnabla_\vecX \vecY
=
X(Y^a)
\vece_a
+Y^b
\underbrace{\nnabla_\vecX \vece_b}_{\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}(\vecX)}\vece_a}
\eeq

Define
\beq
\RR(\vece_x, \vece_y)
(\vece_b)
=
\RR\indices{^a_{xyb}}
\vece_a
\eeq

\beq
[\vece_x, \vece_y]=
C\indices{^f_{xy}} \vece_f
\eeq
By definition

\beq
\nnabla_\vecX
\vece_b
=
\vece_a
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX)
\eeq
Hence

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_c}\vece_b
=
\vece_a
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vece_c)
=
\GGamma\indices{^a_{bc}}
\vece_a
\eeq

We will refer to the constants $\GGamma\indices{^a_{bc}}$ as {\bf generalized \Chris symbols}. They equal 
the normal \Chris symbols
if $\vece_a=
\vecpartial_a$
and $\ul{\chi^a}=
\ul{dx^a}$.

\begin{claim}

Eq.(\ref{eq-rie-inan})
 (index free definition of $\RR$)
implies

\beq
\RR\indices{^a_{xyb}}=
\left[\vece_x(\GGamma\indices{^a_{by}})
+
\GGamma\indices{^f_{by}}
\GGamma\indices{^a_{fx}}
-
(x\swap y)
\right]
- C\indices{^f_{xy}}
\GGamma\indices{^a_{bf}}
\eeq


\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\RR\indices{^a_{xyb}}\vece_a
&=&
\RR(\vece_x, \vece_y)
\vece_b
\\
&=&
[\nnabla_{\vece_x}\nnabla_{\vece_y}\vece_b
-
(x\swap y
)
]
-
\nnabla_{[\vece_x, \vece_y]}\vece_b
\eeqa

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_c}\vece_b
=
\GGamma\indices{^a_{bc}}
\vece_a
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla_{\vece_x}\nnabla_{\vece_y}\vece_b=
\left[
\vece_x(\GGamma\indices{^a_{by}})
+
\GGamma\indices{^f_{by}}
\GGamma\indices{^a_{fx}}
\right]
\vece_a
\eeq

\beqa
\nnabla_{[\vece_x, \vece_y]}\vece_b
&=&
C\indices{^f_{xy}}
\nnabla_{\vece_f}\vece_b
\\
&=&
C\indices{^f_{xy}}
\GGamma\indices{^a_{bf}}
\vece_a
\eeqa


\qed

Define the curvature 2-form by $\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}$ by

\beq
\boxed{
\vece_a\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)
=
\RR(\vecX, \vecY)
(\vece_b)}
\label{eq-ded-curvature-gen}
\eeq

\beq
\vece_a
\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}
(\vece_x,
 \vece_y)
=
\RR(\vece_x, \vece_y)
(\vece_b)
=
\vece_a
\RR\indices{^a_{xyb}}
\eeq

\begin{claim}
Eq.(\ref{eq-ded-curvature-gen}) (definition of curvature in terms of $\RR$) implies

\beq
\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}=
\frac{1}{2}
R\indices{^a_{bxy}}
\ul{\chi^x}
\wedge{\ul{\chi^y}}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof



\beq
\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}=
\frac{1}{2}
\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}
(\vece_x, \vece_y)
\ul{\chi^x}\wedge
\ul{\chi^y}
\eeq

\qed


\begin{claim}
Eq.(\ref{eq-rie-inan})
 (index free definition of $\RR$) and 
Eq.(\ref{eq-ded-curvature-gen}) (definition of curvature in terms of $\RR$) imply
Cartan's Second Structure
Equation

\beq
\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}=
\ul{d\omega\indices{^a_b}}
+
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}
\wedge
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof

\beqa
\vece_a\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)
&=&
\RR(\vecX, \vecY)(\vece_b)
\\
&=&
[\nnabla_\vecX
\nnabla_\vecY 
\vece_b
- (\vecX\swap\vecY)]
-
\nnabla_{[\vecX, \vecY]}
\vece_b
\eeqa

\beq
\nnabla_\vecX \vece_c =
\vece_a\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecX)
\eeq

\beqa
\nnabla_\vecX\nnabla_\vecY \vece_b
&=&
\nnabla_\vecX[\vece_c \ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)]
\\
&=&
\nnabla_\vecX[\vece_c] \ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)
+
\vece_c
\vecX[
 \ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)]
\\
&=&
\vece_a \ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecX)
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)
+
\vece_a
\vecX[
 \ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecY)]
\eeqa

\beq
\nnabla_{[\vecX, \vecY]}
\vece_b
=
\vece_a 
 \ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(
[\vecX, \vecY])
\eeq

\beq
\vece_a\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)=
\vece_a
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\vecX(
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecY))
-\vecY
(\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX))
-
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}([\vecX, \vecY])
\\
+
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecX)
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)
-
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecY)
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}
(\vecX)
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq

\beq
\ul{d\alp}
(\vecX, \vecY)=
\vecX(\ul{\alp}(\vecY))
-
\vecY(\ul{\alp}(\vecX))
-\ul{\alp}
([\vecX, \vecY])
\eeq

\beq
\ul{d
\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)
=
\vecX(
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecY))
-\vecY
(\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX))
-
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}([\vecX, \vecY])
\eeq

\beq
(\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}
\wedge
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}})
(\vecX, \vecY)
=
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecX)
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}(\vecY)
-
\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}(\vecY)
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}}
(\vecX)
\eeq

\beq
\vece_a\ul{\Omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)=
\vece_a
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\ul{d
\omega\indices{^a_b}}(\vecX, \vecY)
\\
+(\ul{\omega\indices{^a_c}}
\wedge
\ul{\omega\indices{^c_b}})
(\vecX, \vecY)
\end{array}
\right.
\eeq

\qed

\beq
\boxed{
\TT(\vecX, \vecY)=
\nnabla_\vecX \vecY
-
\nnabla_\vecY \vecX
-[\vecX, \vecY]}
\label{eq-index-free-torsion}
\eeq

\beq
\TT (\vece_b, \vece_c)= \TT\indices{^a_{bc}}\vece_a
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\TT^a}=
\frac{1}{2}\TT\indices{^a_{bc}}\ul{\chi^b}
\wedge
\ul{\chi^c}
\eeq



\begin{claim}
Eq.(\ref{eq-index-free-torsion})
implies Cartan's
First Structure Equation

\beq
\ul{\TT^a} = 
\ul{d\chi^a}
+\ul{\omega\indices{^a_b}}
\wedge
