We will assume
the reader is familiar, with
the mathematical
concepts of a vector space, Group,
Algebra and Lie algebra.
For some background on those,
see the beginning chapters
of my book, complementary to this one, 
Bayesuvius Quantico ********%\cite{}
What we aim to
do in this section
is to describe
how Lie Groups
can be interpret 
using the
tools and language
of differential geometry 
and p-forms.




$G=$ Lie group. This 
is the manifold $M$
in the
r geometry
interpretation.

$e=$ identity of $G$

$T_g (G)=$ tangent space at point $g$

$\ger{g}=$ Lie algebra. 


\beq
g= \exp(t\vecX)
\eeq
where $g\in G$, $\vecX \in \ger{g}$, $t\geq 0$


{\bf Left and right translations by $g$}
\beq
L_g h = gh, \quad R_g h = hg
\eeq

{\bf Left invariant (LI) vector field} is
a vector field $\vecX\in\ger{X}_{LI}$ for which $\vecX_g\in T_g (G)$
 is defined  for all $g\in G$ 
merely from $\vecX_e\in  T_e(G)$, as follows.

Define a path $g_\gamma(t)\in G$ 
for $t>0$ such that

\beq
g_\gamma(0)=e, \quad
g_\gamma'(0)=\vecX_e
\eeq

\beq
(dL_g)_e: T_e(G)\rarrow T_g(G)
,\quad
(dL_g)_e\vecX_e = \AT{\frac{d\;}{dt}}{t=0} gg_\gamma(t)
\label{eq-g-e-is-ge}
\eeq
For an LI vector field $\vecX$, 
for all $g\in G$,
$\vecX_g$ is defined from $\vecX_e$ by

\beq
\vecX_g = (dL_g)_e\vecX_e
\eeq
We extend the $dL_g$ map to

\beq
(dL_g)_a
\vecX_{a} \eqdef
\vecX_{ga}
\eeq
so the path $h(t)$ is moved to $ah(h)$ before
taking the derivative. Note that the right hand side 
is not $\vecX_{ag}=a\vecX_g$.
Essentially, from Eq.(\ref{eq-g-e-is-ge}),
$(dL_g)_e$ acts like left multiplication by $g$

\beq
g\vecX_e= \vecX_g
\eeq
and we extend that to

\beq
g\vecX_a = \vecX_{ga}
\eeq

For a Lie group,\footnote{$\cong$ means isomorphic, i.e., there exists a 1-1 map between the two sets,
and operations are preserved too.} 

\beq
T_e(G)\eqdef  \{ \vecX_e=g_\gamma'(0)\mid
\text{$g_\gamma(t)\in G$ is a path with $g_\gamma(0)=e$} \}
\eeq

\beq
\ger{X}_{LI}(G) \eqdef \{\vecX\mid \vecX_g=(dL_g)_e \vecX_e\mid g\in G\}
\eeq
\beq
\ger{g}\cong T_e(G)\cong \ger{X}_{LI}(G)
\eeq
The map that makes the sets $T_e(G)$ and 
$\ger{X}_{LI}(G)$ isomorphic is precisely $(dL_g)_e$.

Call $T_g(G)=L_g(T_e(G))$. This reduces to $T_e(G) =T_e(G)$ as expected. Define 

\beq T^p_g(G)= \underbrace{T_g(G)\times\cdots\times T_g(G)}_{\text{$p$ copies}}
\eeq

For 
$\ul{\omega_a}:T^p_a(G)\rarrow \RR$
and $\vecX_a\in T^p_a(G)$,
define the following pullback


\beq
(L_g)^{pb}_a(\ul{\omega_a}(\vecX_a))=
\ul{\omega_{ga}}((L_g)_a \vecX_a)=
\ul{\omega_{ga}}(\vecX_{ga})
\eeq

{\bf Left invariant (LI) p-forms}
$\ul{\omega_a}$ satisfy

\beq
(L_g)^{pb}_a(\ul{\omega_a}(\vecX_a))=
\ul{\omega_{ga}}(\vecX_{ga})=\ul{\omega_a}(\vecX_a)
\eeq
In particular, if $a=e$,

\beq
\ul{\omega_g}(\vecX_g)=\ul{\omega_e}(\vecX_e)
\eeq
Thus,  left invariant p-form
is fully specified  by its value 
at each $\vecX_e\in \ger{g}$.
It's also and \qt{constant}; i.e. the
same for all  $g\in G$.


For $\vecX_e, \vecY_e\in T_e(G) =\ger{g}$, let
$[\vecX_e, \vecY_e]$ stand for the Lie bracket, which is
assumed to be given. Then 
for any LI vector fields
$\vecX, \vecY\in \ger{X}_{LI}$,
define

\beq
[\vecX,  \vecY]_g
\eqdef
\underbrace{
(dL_g)_e
\underbrace{[\vecX_e, \vecY_e]}
_{\in T_e(G)}}_{\in T_g(G)}
\eeq
Note that

\beq
[\vecX, \vecY]_g\neq
[\vecX_g, \vecY_g]
\eeq
because the right hand
side is undefined. Furthermore,
the right hand side
contains only info about 
$\vecX$ and $\vecY$ at $g$,
not in the neighborhood 
of $g$. The later info 
is necessary to compute derivatives.


Let $G$ be an $n$ dim Lie group ($n$
is the dim of the manifold and of $T_e(G)$) with a Lie Algebra $\ger{g}= T_e(G)$.
The {\bf Maurer-Cartan 1-form}
$\pform{\theta}{}$ is defined as 
follows.


\beq
\theta_g: T_p(G)\rarrow \ger{g},\quad \vecX_g \mapsto (dL_{g-1})_g\vecX_g
\eeq

Choose a basis $\{\vecE_\alp\}_{\alp=1}^n$ for $\ger{g}$. Assume

\beq 
[\vecE_\beta, \vecE_\gamma]= C\indices{^\alp_{\beta\gamma}}
\vecE_\alp
\eeq

Expand 

\beq
\pform{\theta}{}=
\pform{\theta^\alp}{}\vecE_\alp
\eeq

\beq
\ul{\theta^\alp}(\vecE_\beta)=
\delta^\alp_\beta
\eeq

\begin{claim}(Maurer-Cartan Equation)


\beq
\ul{d\theta} = -\;\frac{1}{2}
[\ul{\theta}, \ul{\theta}]
\eeq


\beq
\ul{d\theta^\alp}=
-\;\frac{1}{2}
C\indices{^\alp_{\beta\gamma}}
\ul{\theta^\beta}
\wedge
\ul{\theta^\gamma}
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed
