\subsection{Lie Derivatives}

Consider a manifold $M$.

Define a {\bf path} to be a map

\beq
\gamma(\cdot:\pi):[0, 1]\rarrow M,
\quad \tau\mapsto \gamma(\tau;\pi)
\eeq
where $\pi\in M$  is a parameter of the function $\gamma(\cdot;\pi)$.
For example, $\tau$ might be time and 
$\pi$ might be the initial value
of $\gamma(\cdot;\pi)$, i.e., $\pi=\gamma(0;\pi)$.

Define a {\bf flow}  to be a map

\beq
\phi(\cdot;\tau):M \rarrow M,
\quad \pi\mapsto 
\phi(\pi;\tau)=\gamma(\tau;\pi)
\eeq
where $\tau\in[0,1]$  is a parameter of the function $\phi(\cdot;\tau)$.
Thus, $\phi(\cdot;\tau)$ moves points
on the manifold $M$ and is parametrized by a \qt{time} $\tau\in[0,1]$.

Note that we can think of $\phi(\cdot;\tau)$ as being an $M$-slice (at fixed $\tau$)
and of $\gamma(\cdot;\pi)$
as being a $\tau$-slice (at fixed $\pi$)
of a surface
$\Phi:M\times [0,1]\rarrow M$
such that 

\beq
\Phi(\tau, \pi)=\gamma(\tau;\pi)=\phi(\tau;\pi)
\eeq

The Lie derivative $\call_\vecX$
can be defined in terms of pullbacks
a follows. Consider the action of 
$\call_\vecX$ on the metric function
$\metric(\vecU, \vecV)$ as an example.



\beq
\call_{\vecX_\pi}
\metric_\pi(\vecU_\pi ,\vecV_\pi)
=
\left(\AT{\frac{d}{d\tau}}{\tau=0}
\pback{\pi}{\phi(\pi;\tau)}
{\metric_\pi}
\right)(\vecU_\pi ,\vecV_\pi)
=
\left[\AT{\frac{d}{d\tau}}{\tau=0}
\metric_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)}\right]
(\vecU_{\pi},
 \vecV_{\pi})
\eeq
where 
\beq
\phi(\pi;\tau=0)=\pi
\eeq

\beq
\vecX_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)}=\dot{\vgamma}(\tau;\pi)
\eeq

A vector field $\vecX$ is said to be a {\bf Killing field of the metric $\metric$} if

\beq
\call_\vecX \metric=0
\eeq


The Lie derivative
measure how a quantity
changes along a trajectory.
It's defined for vector fields
and dif-forms

The {\bf Lie Derivative $\call_\vecV$  of a vector field
$\vec{U}$} equals

\beq
\call_\vecV\vecU= [\vecU, \vecV]\in\gerX(M)
\eeq

\beq
\begin{array}{cc}
\bcen
\xymatrix{
& \ar[dl]|{\oper{\vecU}}
\\
\call_\vecV \vecU(\vecr)
&&\vecr
\ar[ul]|{\oper{\vecV}}
\ar[dl]|{\oper{\vecU}}
\\
&\ar[ul]|{-\oper{\vecV}}
}\ecen
&
\call_{\vecV}\vecU(\vecr)=
\left[
\oper{\vecV}, \oper{\vecU}
\right](\vecr)\in \RR
\end{array}
\eeq

Coordinate expression

Suppose

\beq
\oper{\vecV} = V^i\partial_i=
\vecV\cdot \nabla,
\quad
\oper{\vecU} =  U^j \partial_j = \vecU\cdot \nabla
\eeq

\beqa
\call_\vecV(\vecU)
&=&
\vecV\cdot \nabla
[
\vecU\cdot \nabla \vecr
]
-
\vecU\cdot \nabla

\vecV\cdot \nabla \vecr

\vecU\cdot \nabla (\vecV )

\nabla \vecr
\\
&=&
[\vecV\cdot \nabla (U^i)-
\vecU\cdot \nabla (V^i )
]
\underbrace{\partial_i \vecr}_{\vecpder{x^i}}
\\
&=&
\oper{\vecV}(\vecU)-
\oper{\vecU}(\vecV)
\eeqa

The {\bf Lie derivative of a dif-form}
is given by
{\bf Cartan's Magic Formula}:

\beq
\begin{array}{cc}
\bcen
\xymatrix{
&\ar[dl]|{d}
\\
\call_\vecX \pform{\omega}{p}
&& \pform{\omega}{p}
\ar[ul]|{i_\vecX}
\ar[dl]|d
\\
&\ar[ul]|{i_\vecX}
}\ecen
&
\boxed
{\call_\vecX = i_\vecX d + d\;i_\vecX}
\end{array}
\eeq
In Cartan's Magic Formula
\begin{itemize}
\item exterior derivative $d(\;)$ gives change at a point.

\item {Lie derivative} $\call_\vecX(\;)$ gives
change along a trajectory
\end{itemize}




$d(\;)$ raises the degree of a dif-form by 1 and $i_\vecX(\;)$
lowers it by one,
so both summands in Cartan Magic Formula
leave the degree
of a dif-form
unchanged.

\begin{itemize}
\item 0-forms:

\beqa
\call_{\vecX}f(\cdot)
&=&
i_\vecX(df) + \cancel{d(i_\vecX f)}^0
\\
&=&
\ul{df}(\vecX)
\\
&=&
\vecX\cdot \nabla f
=  \vecX_{op}(f)
\eeqa
directional derivative

\item 1-form:

Suppose if
$\omega$
and $\vecX$ 
are defined
as follows:

\beq
\ul{\omega}=
\sum_{i=1}^n
\omega_i(x)\ul{dx^i}
\eeq
and

\beq
\vecX =\sum_i X^i(x) \vecpder{x^i}
\eeq

\beq
i_\vecX\ul{\omega}=
\ul{\omega}(\vecX)=
\omega_i X^i
\eeq

\beq
\boxed
{d(i_\vecX \ul{\omega})=
\partial_j
(\omega_i X^i)
\ul{dx^j}}
\label{eq-cmf-part1}
\eeq

\beqa
d\ul{\omega}
&=&
d \left[ \omega_i
\ul {dx^i}\right]
\\
&=&
\partial_j{\omega_i}
\ul{dx^j}\wedge
\ul{dx^i}
\\
&=&
\frac{1}{2}
[\partial_j \omega_i -\partial_i\omega_j]
\ul{dx^j}\wedge
\ul{dx^i}
\eeqa

\beq
i_\vecX
[\ul{dx^j}
\wedge \ul{dx^i}]=
X^j \ul{dx^i}-
X^i \ul{dx^j}
\eeq

\beq
\boxed
{i_\vecX
d\ul{\omega}=
\partial_j\omega_i
\left[
X^j \ul{dx^i}-
X^i\ul{dx^j}
\right]}
\label{eq-cmf-part2}
\eeq

Combining Eq.(\ref{eq-cmf-part1}) and Eq.(\ref{eq-cmf-part2}), we finally get

\beqa
\call_\vecX \pform{\omega}{}
&=&
\left(
X^j\partial_j
\omega_k
+\omega_j \partial_k X^j
\right)
\pform{dx^k}{}
\\
&=&
\vecX\cdot\nabla \pform{\omega}{}
-
\omega_j
\pform{d X^j}{}
\eeqa

\end{itemize}


\subsubsection{Lie derivative of general tensor}\label{sec-Lie-der-tensor}

definition of the Lie derivative is free of
\begin{itemize} \item connection \item metric \item coordinates.
\end{itemize}
 It uses only the action of vector fields and Lie brackets.

\begin{claim}
Suppose $f$ is a
scalar function
(i.e., ${0\choose 0}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX f = \vecX(f)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

\begin{claim}
Suppose $\vecY$ is a
vector field
(i.e., ${1\choose 0}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX \vecY = 
[\vecX, \vecY]
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed


\begin{claim}
Suppose $\pform{\omega}{}$ is a 1-form (i.e., covector, ${0\choose 1}$ tensor).
Then

\beq
\call_\vecX
\ul{\omega}(\vecY)=
\vecX(\ul{\omega}(\vecY))-
\ul{\omega}(
[\vecX, \vecY]
)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed




\begin{claim}

$S$ and $T$,
\beq
\call_\vecX(S\otimes T)=

T
+
S\otimes 
(\call_\vecX T)
\eeq
\end{claim}
\proof
\qed

The following
is valid for LCC 
with possibly a pseudo-\Rien metric.
Specifically, the
following is valid for General
Relativity which is torsion
free and has a pseudo-\Rien
metric (i.e., one with 
one or more negative eigenvalues)

\begin{claim}

\beq
(\call_\vecX  \vecY)=
X^\mu \nnabla_\mu
Y^\nu -
Y^\mu \nnabla_\mu X^\nu
\eeq


\end{claim}
\proof
\beq
\call_\vecX \vecY=
[\oper{\vecX}, \oper{\vecY}]
\eeq

\beq
\vecX = X^\mu \vecpartial_\mu,\quad
\vecY = Y^\nu \vecpartial_\nu
\eeq

\beqa
[\oper{\vecX}, \oper{\vecY}]
&=&
X^\mu\partial_\mu
Y^\nu\partial_\nu\vecr
-
(X\swap Y)
\\
&=&
X^\mu\partial_\mu
Y^\nu-
(X\swap Y)
\eeqa

Next, we replace 
the partial derivatives
by covariant ones because the \Chris symbols
cancel.

\beq
\nnabla_\mu V^\nu=
\partial_\mu V^\nu
+
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu_\lam}}V^\lam
\eeq
for $V=X, Y$


\beq
X^\mu\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu\lam}}Y^\lam
-
(X\swap Y)=0
\eeq
because

\beq
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\mu\lam}} = 
\Gamma\indices{^\nu_{\lam\mu}}
\eeq
The \Chris symbols are symmetric
in $mu, \lam$ because the torsion
is assumed to be zero.

\qed

\begin{claim}
If 
\beq
\ul{\omega}=\omega_\nu \ul{dx^\nu}
\eeq
then

\beq
(\call_\vecX  \ul{\omega})_\nu
=
X^\mu \nnabla_\mu \omega_\nu
+
\omega_\mu \nnabla_\nu X^\mu
\eeq

\end{claim}
\proof

Start from
\beq
(\call_\vecX  \ul{\omega})_\nu
=
X^\mu \partial_\mu \omega_\nu
+
\omega_\mu \partial_\nu X^\mu
\eeq
Now replace the 
partial derivatives 
by covariant derivatives.

\beq
\nnabla_\mu \omega_\nu=
\partial_\mu \omega_\nu -
\Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\mu\nu}}\omega_\lam
\eeq

\beq
\nnabla_\nu X^\mu=
\partial_\nu X^\mu +
\Gamma\indices{^\mu_{\nu\lam}}X^\lam
\eeq


\beq
-X^\mu \Gamma\indices{^\lam_{\mu\nu}}\omega_\lam
+
\omega_\mu
\Gamma\indices{^\mu_{\nu\lam}}X^\lam
=0
\eeq

$u\choose d$ tensor

\nu = (\nu_1\mu_2\cdots\nu_d)
\eeq

\beq
T= T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}
\vecpartial_{\mu_1}\otimes
\vecpartial_{\mu_2}\otimes
\cdots\otimes
\vecpartial_{\mu_u}

\ul{dx}^{\nu_1}\otimes

\beq

\AT{T\indices{^{\mu}_{\nu}}}{\mu_i\rarrow \lam}

\sum_{i=1}^d

\begin{enumerate}
\item

\begin{claim}
Suppose $M=\RR^2$ with Euclidean metric
\beq
\metric(\vecU, \vecV)=
\left[\ul{dx})^2 + (\ul{dy})^2\right
](\vecU, \vecV)= U^1V^1 + U^2V^2 =
\vecU^T \begin{bmatrix}1&0
\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\vecV
\eeq
If

\beq
\vecX = y \vecpder{x}=\begin{bmatrix}y\\0\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
then 
\beq
\call_\vecX\metric = 2\ul{dx}\ul{dy}
\eeq
$\call_\vecX\metric\neq 0$ so 
$\vecX$ is not a Killing field.
This $\vecX$ is called a {\bf shear flow}.
The metric changes along a shear flow.
\end{claim}
\proof

First proof (from pullback definition):

If

\beq
\gamma(\tau;t)=
\begin{bmatrix}x+yt\\y\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
then
\beq 
\pi=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix},
\quad
 \vecX_\gamma = \dot{\vgamma}
\eeq

\beq
\metric_\pi = (\ul{dx})^2  + (\ul{dy})^2,
\quad
\metric_{\gamma(\tau;\pi)} = (\ul{dx} + t\ul{dy})^2 
+ (\ul{dy})^2
\eeq

\beq
\call_\vecX \metric =
\AT{\frac{d}{dt}}{\tau=0}\metric_{\gamma(t)}
=2 \ul{dx}\ul{dy}
\eeq

Second proof (from formula):

\beqa
(\call_\vecX\metric)_{\mu\nu}
&=&
X^\lam \partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}
+
\metric_{\lam\nu}
\partial_\mu X^\lam
+
\metric_{\mu\lam}
\partial_\nu X^\lam
\\
&=&
X^\lam \partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}
+
(\partial_\mu X_\nu + (\mu\swap \nu))
\eeqa

\beq
\partial_\lam \metric_{\mu\nu}=0
\eeq

\beq
\vecX=
\begin{bmatrix}
y\\0
\end{bmatrix}
\implies 
[\partial_\mu X_\nu]
=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1
\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}
\eeq
so 

\beq
[(\call_\vecX\metric)_{\mu\nu}]= 2 
\begin{bmatrix}
0&1
\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}
\eeq

\qed

\vecV)
